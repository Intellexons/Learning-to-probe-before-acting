\noindent\textbf{What this is.}
A dated log of experiments run on the LoRA4VR multi-turn RL loop. Each entry
records what was tried, what happened, and how to reproduce it. Entries are
added as experiments complete. Reproduction references a git commit; the code
at that commit is the exact code that produced the result.

\noindent\textbf{System.} \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} with a LoRA adapter
trained by branched multi-turn GRPO (\texttt{src/branched\_trainer.py}). The
model writes \texttt{policy(state) -> action} Python for the slosh-tank
environment (\texttt{scripts/seed\_tasks/slosh\_tank\_env.py}); the policy is
scored in a sandboxed subprocess. Method and experimental design:
\texttt{docs/paper.tex}.

\section*{2026-10-24 \quad Rung 0 gate: hiding the slosh sensor and the slosh
length does not make the lab necessary}
% ============================================================

\commit{b6485c0} $\cdot$ \textit{hand-written controllers only, CPU; no model
was run}

\subsection*{Setup / motivation}

The ladder restarts with a validity gate: before any model is sampled, show
with hand-written controllers that the scored \texttt{policy(state)} cannot
succeed without information that only the turn-1 lab can provide. Two facts
about the env as shipped were established first (200 seeds per value). With
the actuator gain $\kappa\in\{0.65,0.80,1.00\}$ hidden, a fixed PD controller
with the gain constant hard-coded to $0.65$ succeeds in 100\% of episodes at
every $\kappa$; the shipped ``blind'' reference is the same formula with the
constant at $1.0$ (0\,/\,50.5\,/\,100\%), so the gap it exhibited was a
tuning artifact. With the slosh length $L$ varied from 0.3 to 1.5\,m and the
full state observed, a PD controller that knows nothing about $L$ also
succeeds in 100\% of episodes at every $L$: feedback on $\theta$ does the
probe's job for free.

The redesign under test therefore hid the slosh state. The scored controller
observed only $(t, x, v, \text{goal}, \text{steps\_left})$; $\theta$ and
$\omega$ were visible only to the turn-1 lab env. The hidden parameter was
$L$ (slosh period $T_n = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$), with $\kappa$ fixed at 1. Because
$\theta$ never enters the cart update in \texttt{SloshTankEnv.step}, the
scored controller's inputs are independent of $L$, so no controller, stateful
or not, can identify $L$ in-episode; the only blind strategy is robustness.
The gate measured what robustness costs. A \emph{blind} side (one controller
for every $L$ in the set) and an \emph{informed} side (one controller per
$L$, the upper bound on what a perfect turn-1 measurement could buy) searched
the same five controller classes with the same budget on the same 48 search
seeds: shaped bang-bang moves (none/ZV/ZVD/ZVDD/EI/two-mode shapers plus a
terminal PD), free-form antisymmetric open-loop profiles with 8 segments per
half, pure PD on $(x,v)$, shaped PD with memory, and feedforward plus
tracking feedback. Each job ran 800 random samples and $8\times150$
hill-climbing steps; winners were chosen on the search seeds and reported on
200 fresh seeds per $L$. The gates were fixed beforehand: G1, informed
$\geq 90\%$ at every $L$; G2, informed mean minus blind mean $\geq 40$
percentage points; G3, a hand-written two-turn solution (lab measurement,
then controller) $\geq 95\%$ on fresh seeds. Twelve configurations were run
(1.3 CPU-hours, 7 minutes on 11 cores).

G1 passed everywhere. G2 failed in every configuration. The blind winner was
always a free-form open-loop profile, typically a short bang-bang convolved
with a two-frequency shaper, whose success on the continuous ranges was 100\%
over uniformly drawn $L$. G3 passed at 100\% for every $L$ in every
configuration with at least 90 steps: the lab code applied a four-step pulse
and read the period off the zero crossings of $\theta$ to within 0.2\%
(e.g.\ 4.008\,s measured against 4.012\,s true), chose the move acceleration
on lab goals disjoint from the scoring goals, and the turn-2 controller was a
ZV-shaped bang-bang with the two constants filled in. At 80 steps G3 fell to
90.5\% at $L=4$.

\subsection*{Interpretation}

The premise of the redesign holds, but an unobservable parameter is not the
same as a necessary measurement. Robust input shaping over $\pm20\%$ of
frequency uncertainty costs little time, and the optimizer found profiles
that keep the residual slosh inside the tolerance for all three periods at
once. The $+58$\,pp gap seen in the pilot came from a controller family that
contained only classical single-frequency shapers; once the blind side was
allowed free-form profiles it vanished. A gap appears only when the budget is
squeezed below what the longest period needs, and there it also hurts the
informed side (G3 at 80 steps), so it is a budget artifact rather than an
information gap.

Three constraints on any future design follow. First, anything the scored
controller observes that depends on the hidden quantity reveals it within a
step or two, because the dynamics are deterministic; the hidden quantity must
affect only variables the scored controller cannot see. Second, if the hidden
quantity admits a robust compromise, the lab buys nothing; frequency
uncertainty of this width admits one. Third, a scale-like parameter such as
the actuator gain admits no compromise under a fixed horizon, but the env's
termination on first success lets a blind open-loop plan test hypotheses in
sequence: with $\kappa\in\{0.65,0.8,1.0\}$ a plan that stops at planned
positions $d$, $1.25d$ and $1.54d$ succeeds under each value in turn. Success
would have to be judged at the final step.

\subsection*{Hypotheses / next steps}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Run the same gate on a dead-reckoning variant: the scored controller
  observes only $(t, \text{goal}, \text{steps\_left})$, the gain (and
  optionally the period) is hidden, and success is evaluated at the last step
  with no early termination. The blind side can then be right for at most one
  gain value, so the gap exists by construction; the gate would confirm the
  informed side reaches $\geq 90\%$ and the hand-written two-turn solution
  $\geq 95\%$.
\item If it passes, push the two-turn solution through the real runner with
  the observation filter, a lab proxy that hides \texttt{env.hidden}, a lab
  seed disjoint from the scoring seeds, and scoring on at least four goals.
\item Check at Rung 2 that a frozen model does not already solve the lab
  step, since measuring a gain takes one line of code.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Open questions}

Whether the no-sensor story is physically acceptable to a reviewer; whether
judging success at the final step sparsifies the reward enough to hurt
single-turn RL at Rung 3; whether slosh is still worth keeping once the gain
carries the information gap.

\section*{2026-10-24 \quad Rung 0 gate, second pass: dead reckoning with a
hidden gain passes, provided success is judged at the final step}
% ============================================================

\commit{3dc6581} $\cdot$ \textit{hand-written controllers only, CPU; no model
was run}

\subsection*{Setup / motivation}

Following item 1 of the previous entry, the scored controller was stripped of
every sensor: it observed only $(t, \text{goal}, \text{steps\_left})$. The
actuator gain $\kappa$ was hidden (discrete $\{0.65, 0.8, 1.0\}$ or continuous
$U[0.6,1.0]$), alone with the shipped slosh length $L=0.7$\,m or together
with a hidden $L$. Nothing the scored controller sees depends on either
value, so the lab is the only channel, and a gain admits no robust
compromise: a plan that lands on the goal under one gain lands short or long
under another. One loophole remained. The shipped env ends the episode at the
first step that satisfies the success test, so a blind plan can stop at the
goal position implied by each gain hypothesis in turn and be credited whichever
is true. A thin subclass therefore judged success at the final step only and
paid the bonus only there; the shipped rule was kept as a control. The
protocol was the one of the previous entry (same classes and budget on both
sides, 48 search seeds, 200 fresh seeds per hidden value, gates G1--G3 fixed
in advance), restricted to the open-loop classes since there is no feedback
to use, plus a new multi-stop class (back-to-back shaped moves through
planned positions, with an idle dwell) so the blind side could exploit the
loophole wherever the rules allowed it. Six configurations were run with the
final-step rule and two as the control (126 and 24 jobs; 6 minutes on 11
cores).

$\kappa\in\{0.65,0.8,1\}$, $L\in\{2,3,4\}$ & 105 & final step & 33 & 100 & $+67$ & pass / pass \\
$\kappa\in\{0.65,0.8,1\}$, $L\in\{2,3,4\}$ & 120 & final step & 33 & 100 & $+66$ & pass / pass \\
$\kappa\sim U[0.6,1]$, $L\sim U[2,4]$    & 105 & final step & 33 (range 24) & 100 & $+66$ & pass / pass \\
\midrule
$\kappa\in\{0.65,0.8,1\}$, $L=0.7$       & 105 & first success (control) & 71 & 99 & $+28$ & pass / FAIL \\
$\kappa\in\{0.65,0.8,1\}$, $L=0.7$       & 135 & first success (control) & 100 & 100 & $0$ & pass / FAIL \\

Under the final-step rule the blind winner succeeded for exactly one gain
value (100\,/\,0\,/\,0 across the three) in every discrete configuration and
for 18\% of a continuous gain range; the informed side was at 99--100\% for
every hidden pair, including the nine $(\kappa, L)$ pairs. Under the shipped
rule the blind winner was always the multi-stop class with three stops at
planned positions $d$, $1.26d$ and $1.45d$: 13\,/\,100\,/\,100 at 105 steps
and 100\,/\,100\,/\,99 at 135 steps. G3 passed at 100\% for every hidden pair
in all four configurations tried ($L=0.7$ and $L\in\{2,3,4\}$ at 90--120
steps). The lab code recovered $\kappa$ exactly from the velocity after one
full-throttle step and the slosh period to within 0.3\% from a four-step
pulse; the turn-2 controller was the ZV-shaped bang-bang of the previous entry
with the gain folded into its scaling and no terminal feedback.

\subsection*{Interpretation}

The gap now exists by construction rather than by search: with no field
sensors the scored controller cannot learn the gain, and a gain cannot be
hedged, so the blind side is capped at one hypothesis (a third of a
three-value set, a tolerance-width sliver of a continuous range). The
measurement is trivial for the lab and useless to withhold. The control
shows that the final-step rule is load-bearing and not cosmetic: given 135
steps the shipped first-success rule lets a blind plan reach 100\% by visiting
three candidate positions in sequence. Hiding $L$ as well adds nothing to the
gap (the gain already caps the blind side) but makes turn 2 harder and turn 1
richer, two quantities to measure in one lab episode. The continuous gain
range is preferable to the discrete set: it leaves nothing to memorize and
drives the blind side to a few percent.

\subsection*{Hypotheses / next steps}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Env and runner changes for this variant: an observation filter so the
  scored \texttt{policy(state)} receives only $(t, \text{goal},
  \text{steps\_left})$ while the turn-1 lab env keeps the full observation; the
  final-step success rule with the bonus paid only at the end; $L$ added to
  \texttt{HiddenParams}; a lab proxy that exposes \texttt{reset}, \texttt{step}
  and \texttt{observe} but not \texttt{env.hidden}; a lab seed disjoint from
  the scoring seeds; scoring on at least four goals.
\item Rung 0b: the hand-written two-turn solution through the real
  \texttt{execute\_turn} path at $\geq 95\%$.
\item Rungs 1--2 as planned, with the explicit risk that a frozen model solves
  the one-line gain measurement from the prompt alone; if it does, the ladder
  tests competence rather than probing, and the paper's framing follows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Open questions}

Whether the final-step rule, which withholds the bonus until the end,
sparsifies the reward enough to slow single-turn RL at Rung 3 (the shaping
terms remain per step). Whether to present the task as open-loop feedforward
actuation calibrated on a bench, and whether a reviewer accepts that
framing. Whether $L$ should stay hidden or be fixed and known.

\section*{2026-10-24 \quad New environment: a seeded cooling crystallizer
with a hidden metastable limit passes the Rung 0 gate}
% ============================================================

\commit{038e6d7} $\cdot$ \textit{hand-written controllers only, CPU; no model
was run}

\subsection*{Setup / motivation}

The slosh tank was dropped after the two previous entries. Its hidden numbers
were dynamics parameters, visible in the first steps of the final run, and the
only variant that passed the gate did so by removing sensors. The rule that
replaced it: the probe run is the same env with the same actions and the same
readings as the final run, so the hidden quantity must be one that a probe run
can reveal through the failure flag, the end-of-episode outcome or free
repetition, and that a single scored episode cannot. A limit that is only found
by crossing it is the cleanest case, because crossing it in the final run is
the failure. The seeded batch cooling crystallizer
(\texttt{scripts/seed\_tasks/crystallizer\_env.py}) implements it. A saturated
solution carrying seed crystals is cooled from about 60 to about 20\,$^\circ$C;
the cooling rate (jacket limit 1.5\,$^\circ$C/min, 20\,s steps, 200\,min
horizon) is the only control; the supersaturation $\sigma = c/c^*(T) - 1$
drives seed growth ($G = k_g\sigma^{1.5}$) and is consumed by it; above the
hidden metastable limit $\sigma_{\max}\sim U[0.08, 0.25]$ a shower of new
nuclei ends the batch. The observation is $(t, T, c, L, T_{\text{target}},
\text{steps\_left})$; $\sigma_{\max}$ appears nowhere except in the shower
test, and two batches with limits 0.10 and 0.25 gave identical observations
under identical actions until one showered. Success is reaching the target
temperature equilibrated ($\sigma\le 0.02$); reward $-0.1$ per step, $+120$ on
success, $-80$ on a shower. The fastest safe schedule rides just below the
limit and accelerates as the crystals grow (the classical programmed-cooling
result), so the final run needs both the limit and a real controller. The
protocol was that of the previous entries: five controller families
(one-step-lookahead supersaturation tracking, linear cooling, power-law
cooling, piecewise-linear profile, power-law profile with a supersaturation
cap), 300 random samples plus 120 hill-climbing steps from the top six, 48
search seeds, 200 fresh seeds, both sides maximizing mean env reward. Scores:
success; fast success, i.e.\ finishing within $1.25\times$ the time of the
oracle that rides exactly at $\sigma_{\max}$; and normalized time (oracle
steps over own steps, 0 on failure). Gates fixed in advance: G1 informed fast
success $\ge 90\%$ at every anchor value; G2 blind fast success at least
40\,pp below; G3 hand-written probe run plus final run $\ge 95\%$.

\subsection*{Results}

\begin{center}\small
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{llrrrrrrl}
\toprule
hidden $\sigma_{\max}$ & time wt & blind fast (\%) & blind norm.\ time & blind reward & inf.\ fast (\%) & inf.\ norm.\ time & inf.\ reward & G1 / G2 (gap) \\
\midrule
$U[0.08,0.25]$, anchors 0.08 / 0.165 / 0.25 & 0.1 & 7.5 (100 / 0 / 0) & 0.50 & 91 & 100 & 1.00 & 91 / 108 / 110 & pass / pass ($+92$) \\
$\{0.08, 0.12, 0.18, 0.25\}$                & 0.1 & 25 (100 / 0 / 0 / 0) & 0.58 & 91 & 100 & 1.00 & 91 / 103 / 109 / 110 & pass / pass ($+75$) \\
$U[0.08,0.25]$ (sensitivity)                & 0.3 & 11 (0 / 0 / 0)   & 0.52 & 38 & 100 & 1.00 & 33 / 83 / 91 & pass / pass ($+89$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

In both configurations the blind winner was the tracking family riding at
$\sigma_t\approx 0.08$, the lowest limit in the range, which never showers and
needs about 100 minutes for every batch; the informed side finished in 33 to
98 minutes depending on the limit. The open-loop families could not reach fast
success even when informed ($\le 10\%$), so feedback on the concentration is
needed to ride the limit. G3 passed at 100\% fast success at every anchor in
both configurations: the probe run cooled at the jacket limit until the shower,
then in two more batches crept up in eighths of the remaining bracket, and
recovered the limit to within 0.0002 (0.004 at the top of the range); the final
run rode at 98\% of the recovered value.

\subsection*{Interpretation}

The gap comes from physics and not from a narrow controller family or a
withheld sensor. A limit cannot be learned without crossing it, crossing it in
the final run is the failure, and feedback on every reading is allowed. Under
the env reward the blind side's best policy is caution, so the probe run buys
time rather than success: two to three times faster on the easy batches, which
is 12 to 19 reward points out of 120 at a time weight of 0.1. Tripling the time
weight raises that to about 32 points over the range but the blind side then
starts to gamble ($\sigma_t\approx 0.084$, showering the 4\% of batches whose
limit lies below it). Compared with the slosh tank, nothing was hidden from the
final run that a plant would have, and the probe's information channel, the
failure flag, is one both runs share.

\subsection*{Hypotheses / next steps}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Runner: the scenario carries a \texttt{hidden} dict and the templates
  build the hidden parameters from it; the probe-run env is wrapped so that
  reset, step, observe, the success and safety tests and the config are
  reachable but the hidden parameters are not; the probe-run seed differs
  from the scoring seed (same limit, different batch); a task builder with
  the prompt text; tests.
\item Rung 0b: the hand-written probe run and final run through the real
  \texttt{execute\_turn} path at $\ge 95\%$.
\item Fix the time weight (0.1 or 0.3) before any training run; it sets how
  much of the reward the probe is worth against how much the blind side is
  tempted to gamble.
\item Rungs 1--2 as planned. The probe is a few lines; a frozen model may
  already write it, and the ladder will say so.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Open questions}

Whether 600 steps per episode is acceptable for the branched-rollout cost.
Whether the growth constant $k_g$ should also vary by batch; it is identifiable
from the readings of the final run, which would show the contrast between a
quantity that needs the probe run and one that does not inside a single env.
Whether to add a jacket lag, which would make riding the limit harder and force
a margin.
